# Gaara (source)

Source: [Naruto Fandom wiki — Gaara](https://naruto.fandom.com/wiki/Gaara)
Raw file: `raw/Gaara.md`

## Summary

Exhaustive biography of Gaara: his traumatic childhood as Sunagakure's involuntary
jinchūriki of Shukaku, his transformation from a feared killer into Suna's Fifth
Kazekage under [[Naruto Uzumaki]]'s influence, and his ongoing role as one of the
Five Kage through the *New Era*.

## Key takeaways

- Sealed with Shukaku before birth by his father's order, Gaara's mother Karura dies
  giving birth to him; his uncle Yashamaru is later sent to assassinate him, telling
  Gaara (falsely) that neither parent ever loved him — the trauma that turns young
  Gaara into a killer who values only himself.
- His defeat at the hands of Naruto Uzumaki (access restricted — see his page) during
  the Chūnin Exams/Konoha Crush becomes Gaara's turning point: he spends the rest of
  the series consciously emulating Naruto's example, eventually becoming Suna's Fifth
  Kazekage and a driving force behind Five Kage cooperation.
- Details the Kazekage Rescue Mission from Gaara's side (captured by Akatsuki's
  Deidara and Sasori, revived by Chiyo's self-sacrifice) and his role at the Five Kage
  Summit and Fourth Shinobi World War, including commanding the Allied Forces' Fourth
  Division alongside Shikamaru Nara.
- Post-war: marries no one by arrangement (the *Gaara Hiden* marriage plot resolves
  with him helping the intended bride elope instead) but adopts Shinki, a boy he finds
  struggling with an uncontrolled kekkei genkai. Attends Naruto and Hinata's wedding.
- *New Era*: remains a recurring Kage-level figure through the Ōtsutsuki-era arcs
  (Momoshiki, Urashiki) and into the Kawaki Arc, where he argues Kawaki should be
  treated with sympathy rather than as a weapon — his own jinchūriki experience
  informing the position. Reaches the *New Era: Part II* Boruto's Return Arc, where he
  is severely injured defending Suna from the God Tree Matsuri and left comatose/sealed
  until freed and returned to Suna to recover — the most current status this source
  covers.
